On small tools and sharp edges

May 15, 2026

The software I rely on most is almost always small. A focused text editor. A handful of shell scripts I wrote in an afternoon. A single-page HTML site like this one. None of it tries to be everything.

Small tools have sharp edges. If you use them wrong, they will not save you. You can delete the wrong file. You can write CSS that breaks on a phone. You can ship a typo to production. The big platforms try to round those edges off by adding guardrails, and the guardrails inevitably get in the way.

I've come around to thinking the edges are the point. They force you to understand what you're doing, even just a little. The understanding compounds. A year in, you can think clearly about a whole layer of the stack that used to be magic.

The trade-off is real. Small tools cost more attention up front. They don't come with a friendly onboarding flow. But the ones that survive in your toolbox tend to stay for years, and they don't surprise you in the way larger systems do.

If you're choosing between a tool that does one thing and a tool that does twelve, I'd start with the one. You can always reach for more later. It's much harder to go the other way.
